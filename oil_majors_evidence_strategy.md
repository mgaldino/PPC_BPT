# PPC Evidence: Oil Majors' Internal Strategy & ESG Documents (Post-2020)

## Context

**Hypotheses** (from Fairfield & Charman 2025, reanalyzing Vormedal et al. 2020):

- **H_SA** (Strategic Accommodation): Oil majors support moderate carbon pricing to hedge against / preclude more radical regulation (defensive motive).
- **H_CA** (Competitive Advantage): Oil majors support carbon pricing because it creates competitive advantages -- gas over coal, market positioning (offensive motive).

**F&C posterior**: ~4 dB for H_SA (~71% probability).

**Holdout principle**: All evidence below postdates F&C's analysis window and was not used in their assessment.

---

## Evidence Item 1: The Greenpeace/Unearthed ExxonMobil Sting (June 2021)

### Description

In June 2021, Greenpeace's investigative unit Unearthed released undercover video of Keith McCoy, ExxonMobil's senior director for federal relations, speaking candidly to activists posing as job recruiters. McCoy stated that ExxonMobil's public support for a carbon tax was a cynical "talking point" because the company knew it would never be enacted:

> "Nobody is going to propose a tax on all Americans and the cynical side of me says, yeah, we kind of know that but it gives us a talking point that we can say, well what is ExxonMobil for? Well, we're for a carbon tax."

When asked "So it's basically never going to happen right, is the calculation?", McCoy replied: "Yeah. No it's not, it's not."

McCoy also described working with "shadow groups" and lobbying senators to weaken climate elements of Biden's infrastructure plan.

### Sources

- [Inside Exxon's playbook -- Unearthed/Greenpeace](https://unearthed.greenpeace.org/2021/06/30/exxon-climate-change-undercover/)
- [NPR: Exxon Lobbyist Caught On Video](https://www.npr.org/2021/07/01/1012138741/exxon-lobbyist-caught-on-video-talks-about-undermining-bidens-climate-push)
- [CNN: Undercover Exxon video reveals an anti-climate campaign](https://www.cnn.com/2021/07/01/business/exxon-tape-video-keith-mccoy/index.html)
- [CBS News: Exxon faces heat](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/exxon-mobil-greenpeace-video-lobbyist-carbon-tax-talking-point/)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_SA strongly
- **Estimated WoE**: +6 to +8 dB for H_SA
- **Diagnosticity**: Very high. This is a rare, unguarded internal admission that carbon pricing support is a strategic shield ("talking point"), not a genuine policy preference. The defensive logic (support something you know won't happen to appear proactive) maps directly onto H_SA. Under H_CA, if carbon pricing genuinely created competitive advantages, the company would want it to pass -- not celebrate its impossibility. One caveat: McCoy's views may not represent the full range of internal thinking, and ExxonMobil's CEO publicly disavowed his comments.

## Evidence Item 2: API's Carbon Pricing "Support" vs. Lobbying Record

### Description

In March 2021, the American Petroleum Institute (API) -- the oil industry's most powerful lobbying group, whose members include ExxonMobil, Shell, BP, and Chevron -- announced for the first time that it would support setting a price on carbon. However, congressional investigations revealed that less than 0.4% of the four major oil companies' and API's lobbying on specific legislation since 2011 was directed at carbon pricing legislation. API spent over $100 million lobbying the federal government over this period, overwhelmingly on issues unrelated to carbon pricing. InfluenceMap's analysis noted that "embracing carbon pricing does not necessarily translate into support for effective climate policy until there is a specific piece of legislation or regulation to implement."

API simultaneously lobbied to weaken the Methane Emissions Reduction Program under the Inflation Reduction Act and pushed for greater incentives for fossil gas.

### Sources

- [InfluenceMap: American Petroleum Institute's 'carbon price' policy](https://influencemap.org/pressrelease/American-Petroleum-Institute-s-carbon-price-policy-2e62fb8756be1dc5c5e40f9aff65aebf)
- [House Oversight Committee: Lobbying analysis reveals public praise not backed by meaningful action](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/committee-analysis-of-fossil-fuel-industry-s-lobbying-reveals-public-praise-for)
- [Grist: Oil's biggest lobbying group killed carbon prices. Now it supports them?](https://grist.org/energy/oils-biggest-lobbying-group-killed-carbon-pricing-now-it-supports-them/)
- [CNN: America's most powerful oil lobby is changing its tune on a carbon tax](https://www.cnn.com/2021/03/25/business/oil-lobby-carbon-pricing-api/index.html)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_SA moderately-to-strongly
- **Estimated WoE**: +4 to +6 dB for H_SA
- **Diagnosticity**: High. Under H_SA, carbon pricing support is a rhetorical shield, not an action priority -- consistent with near-zero lobbying expenditure on actual carbon pricing bills. Under H_CA, one would expect substantial lobbying effort to actually enact carbon pricing to capture the competitive benefits. The enormous gap between public positioning and lobbying allocation is diagnostic.

## Evidence Item 3: Shell's Carbon Pricing Advocacy Serves Gas-over-Coal Strategy

### Description

Corporate Europe Observatory's analysis of Shell's lobbying reveals that Shell's primary motivation for advocating carbon pricing in the EU was to help its gas business out-compete coal in electricity generation, not to drive deep decarbonization. Shell lobbied the EU to treat gas as the "bridge fuel" away from coal, writing to former Commission President Barroso: "Gas is good for Europe, and Europe is good at gas." In October 2011, Shell proposed to the Commission that it should scrap EU renewables and energy efficiency targets in favour of a single greenhouse gas target, with the EU ETS as the sole policy instrument -- a framework that would advantage gas over both coal and renewables.

Shell's 2021 Tax Contribution Report and lobbying disclosures confirm that the company advocated for carbon pricing to apply "to as many sectors as possible" and to "increase over time," while simultaneously positioning LNG as its major growth business (targeting 30% growth by 2030).

Shell's carbon costs under the EU ETS were approximately $493 million in 2022, projected to rise to $1.5 billion by 2032 -- a material but manageable cost for a company that generated $40+ billion in annual profit.

### Sources

- [Corporate Europe Observatory: The future according to Shell](https://corporateeurope.org/en/future-according-shell)
- [Shell: Carbon pricing advocacy page](https://www.shell.com/sustainability/advocacy-and-political-activity/carbon-pricing.html)
- [Shell Tax Contribution Report 2021: Carbon pricing](https://reports.shell.com/tax-contribution-report/2021/advocacy/carbon-pricing.html)
- [S&P Global: Shell warns of ballooning carbon costs](https://www.spglobal.com/commodityinsights/en/market-insights/latest-news/oil/031023-shell-warns-of-ballooning-carbon-costs-climate-risks-to-its-oil-gas-business)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_CA moderately (but with a twist)
- **Estimated WoE**: +2 to +3 dB for H_CA
- **Diagnosticity**: Moderate. Shell's explicit gas-over-coal framing is consistent with H_CA's core mechanism (carbon pricing creates competitive advantages for gas). However, the simultaneous push to eliminate renewables targets and rely solely on ETS-based policy suggests the advocacy is also strategically defensive (H_SA) -- carbon pricing as a substitute for more stringent regulation. This evidence is partially consistent with both hypotheses, reducing its diagnosticity. On balance, the gas competitive advantage framing is more explicitly H_CA, but the anti-renewables-target lobbying introduces an H_SA element.

## Evidence Item 4: ExxonMobil's Low Carbon Solutions (LCS) Business and CCS Strategy

### Description

In 2021, ExxonMobil established its Low Carbon Solutions (LCS) business focused on carbon capture and storage (CCS), low-carbon hydrogen, ammonia, lithium, and advanced fuels. The company explicitly frames carbon pricing as essential to the commercial viability of this new business line. At a carbon price of $100/ton, CCS can deliver up to 25% of national emissions reductions and creates incentives for industrial firms to use ExxonMobil's services. The company expects LCS to grow earnings by $2 billion by 2030.

The Global Solutions Initiative analysis describes this as a "strategic business move": carbon pricing creates a new market for ExxonMobil's CCS capabilities and makes "blue" hydrogen (from natural gas with CCS) cost-competitive, directly benefiting ExxonMobil's gas and hydrogen operations.

CEO Darren Woods has advocated for a carbon price of at least $100/ton.

### Sources

- [Global Solutions Initiative: ExxonMobil's Support for Carbon Pricing: A Strategic Business Move](https://www.global-solutions-initiative.org/article/exxonmobil-supports-a-price-on-carbon-its-good-for-their-business/)
- [ExxonMobil: Why ExxonMobil supports carbon pricing](https://corporate.exxonmobil.com/news/viewpoints/supports-carbon-pricing)
- [ExxonMobil: Growing Low Carbon Solutions](https://corporate.exxonmobil.com/sustainability-and-reports/advancing-climate-solutions/growing-low-carbon-solutions)
- [ExxonMobil: A breakout year for CCS](https://corporate.exxonmobil.com/news/viewpoints/a-breakout-year-for-our-carbon-capture-and-storage-business)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_CA moderately
- **Estimated WoE**: +3 to +4 dB for H_CA
- **Diagnosticity**: Moderate-to-high. ExxonMobil's explicit framing of carbon pricing as enabling a profitable new business line aligns directly with H_CA (competitive advantage through market creation). However, juxtaposed with Evidence Item 1 (the McCoy sting), a more complex picture emerges: ExxonMobil's public-facing strategy documents promote H_CA logic, while an unguarded internal admission reveals H_SA logic. This tension itself is informative -- it suggests H_CA framing may be the external narrative while H_SA is the operative internal logic, or that different parts of the organization hold different views.

## Evidence Item 5: The 2023--2025 "Great Retreat" from Climate Commitments

### Description

Between 2023 and 2025, European oil majors systematically retreated from their climate pledges:

**BP** (February 2025): CEO Murray Auchincloss declared that BP's faith in the green energy transition was "misplaced" and the company had gone "too far, too fast." BP cut annual renewable investment from ~$5 billion to $1.5-2 billion, increased oil and gas spending to $10 billion/year, scrapped its target to increase renewable generation 20-fold by 2030, and weakened its emissions reduction target from 25-40% to 20-30% by 2030. The reversal was partly driven by activist investor Elliott Management.

**Shell** (June 2023 / March 2024): CEO Wael Sawan dropped Shell's target to cut oil production and confirmed fossil fuel production would be maintained through 2030. Shell abandoned its 2035 net-zero target, weakened its 2030 goal, confirmed it would no longer lead new offshore wind developments, and planned to grow LNG by 30% by 2030. Shell also formally withdrew from the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi).

**Equinor** (2024): Halved its renewable investment from $10 billion to $5 billion over two years, lowered expected renewables capacity to 10-12 GW by 2030, and stepped back from SBTi.

Industry-wide, BP, Shell, and Equinor reduced low-carbon spending by 8% in 2024.

### Sources

- [Fortune: BP unveils strategy shakeup](https://fortune.com/europe/2025/02/26/bp-energy-giant-unveils-strategy-shakeup-amid-global-energy-transition-murray-auchincloss/)
- [Al Jazeera: BP drops climate targets in pivot back to oil and gas](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/2/26/bp-drops-climate-targets-in-switch-back-to-oil-and-gas)
- [Carbon Brief: Shell abandons 2035 emissions target](https://www.carbonbrief.org/shell-abandons-2035-emissions-target-and-weakens-2030-goal/)
- [Sustainability Magazine: Equinor Halves Green Spend](https://sustainabilitymag.com/articles/why-is-equinor-halving-renewables-spend-growing-oil-gas)
- [Fast Company: BP and Shell backtracked on climate goals in 2024](https://www.fastcompany.com/91253071/bp-shell-backtracked-climate-goals-2024-heres-why)
- [Carbon Tracker: Backing Petroleum -- BP's transition U-turn](https://carbontracker.org/bp-u-turn/)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_SA strongly
- **Estimated WoE**: +5 to +7 dB for H_SA
- **Diagnosticity**: High. The retreat reveals that prior climate pledges (including carbon pricing advocacy as part of transition strategies) were not driven by genuine commercial excitement about competitive advantages in a low-carbon economy (H_CA). If H_CA were the primary motivation, companies would double down on renewables and carbon pricing advocacy as long-term profit opportunities. Instead, when short-term fossil fuel profits surged and regulatory pressure modestly eased, companies rapidly abandoned green investments. This pattern is consistent with H_SA: climate positioning was a strategic hedge that could be unwound when the perceived regulatory threat diminished. The simultaneous maintenance of carbon pricing rhetoric while slashing actual transition spending is particularly diagnostic.

## Evidence Item 6: U.S. Congressional Investigation -- "Denial, Disinformation, and Doublespeak" (April 2024)

### Description

A joint Senate Budget Committee / House Oversight Committee investigation (led by Sen. Whitehouse and Rep. Raskin) released a staff report in April 2024 titled "Denial, Disinformation, and Doublespeak: Big Oil's Evolving Efforts to Avoid Accountability for Climate Change." Based on internal documents obtained from ExxonMobil, Chevron, BP, and Shell, the report found:

1. **Private skepticism of Paris Agreement**: Companies internally expressed skepticism of the Paris agreement, federal climate regulations, and their own net-zero goals, even while publicly voicing support.
2. **Carbon pricing as rhetorical shield**: Companies opted for "low-hanging fruit" in their public commitments, including "simple statements of support for climate science or carbon pricing," while actual lobbying focused elsewhere.
3. **CCS/CCUS acknowledged as unviable**: Internal BP documents reveal that CCS "at scale is not economically viable against a free-to-pollute business model" and that a "robust carbon price [would be] needed to stimulate CCUS deployment" -- yet companies publicly promoted CCS as a climate solution regardless.
4. **Methane deception**: Companies privately downplayed serious climate harms from methane emissions while publicly positioning natural gas as a climate-friendly fuel.
5. **Technology greenwashing**: Companies knew that technologies they publicly promoted as climate solutions (algae-based biofuels, CCS) were "neither as green nor as feasible as they promised."

### Sources

- [Senate Budget Committee press release](https://www.budget.senate.gov/chairman/newsroom/press/new-joint-bicameral-staff-report-reveals-big-oils-campaign-of-climate-denial-disinformation-and-doublespeak/)
- [Full report (PDF)](https://www.budget.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/fossil_fuel_report1.pdf)
- [House Oversight Committee press release](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/new-joint-bicameral-staff-report-reveals-big-oils-campaign-climate-denial)
- [DeSmog: Congressional Investigation Reveals New Evidence](https://www.desmog.com/2024/05/01/congressional-investigation-sheldon-whitehouse-fossil-fuel-industry-report-carbon-emissions-contribute-to-climate-change-senate-budget-committee-jamie-raskin/)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_SA strongly
- **Estimated WoE**: +5 to +7 dB for H_SA
- **Diagnosticity**: High. Internal documents showing private skepticism of net-zero goals combined with public support for carbon pricing directly support H_SA's mechanism: carbon pricing advocacy as a strategic shield against more radical regulation, not as a genuine business opportunity. The finding that carbon pricing was "low-hanging fruit" in public positioning while lobbying focused elsewhere is a near-direct confirmation of the H_SA logic. The CCS admission is also diagnostic: if companies privately acknowledge CCS is unviable without a robust carbon price they simultaneously lobby to prevent, the carbon pricing advocacy is performative.

## Evidence Item 7: InfluenceMap's "Big Oil's Real Agenda" Reports (2022-2023)

### Description

InfluenceMap published a series of reports documenting the gap between oil majors' public climate rhetoric and their actual lobbying behavior:

1. **Capital expenditure**: Only 12% of the five major oil companies' 2022 CAPEX was dedicated to "low carbon" activities, despite public rhetoric about transition leadership.
2. **Paris non-alignment**: None of the five companies aligned their climate policy engagement with Paris Agreement goals.
3. **Trade association networks**: Companies retained dense global networks of industry associations that actively opposed Paris-aligned climate policies, even as companies themselves made pro-climate public statements.
4. **Record profits, doubled-down fossils**: In February 2023, after announcing record-breaking profits, oil majors chose to double down on fossil fuels, not accelerate their transition. InfluenceMap argued that companies' climate policy engagement "continues to be a more accurate proxy for the companies' climate strategies than top-line statements."

### Sources

- [InfluenceMap: Big Oil's Real Agenda on Climate Change 2022](https://influencemap.org/report/Big-Oil-s-Agenda-on-Climate-Change-2022-19585)
- [InfluenceMap: Fossil Fuel Climate Lobbying Update #5 (Dec 2022/Jan 2023)](https://influencemap.org/report/Fossil-Fuel-Climate-Lobbying-Update-December-2022-January-2023-21039)
- [InfluenceMap: Fossil Fuels Climate Lobbying Update #6 (Feb 2023)](https://influencemap.org/briefing/Fossil-Fuels-Climate-Lobbying-Update-February-2023-21495)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_SA moderately
- **Estimated WoE**: +3 to +5 dB for H_SA
- **Diagnosticity**: Moderate-to-high. The systematic pattern of public-facing climate rhetoric (including carbon pricing support) combined with lobbying behavior that opposes effective climate policy is consistent with H_SA's strategic accommodation logic. Under H_CA, companies would be expected to lobby for strong climate policy that creates genuine competitive advantages. The 12% low-carbon CAPEX figure is particularly telling -- if carbon pricing created real competitive advantages, rational firms would allocate significantly more capital to capture them.

## Evidence Item 8: TotalEnergies Greenwashing Conviction (October 2025)

### Description

On October 23, 2025, the Judicial Tribunal of Paris found that TotalEnergies and its French subsidiary engaged in misleading environmental advertising. TotalEnergies' claims about its "ambition to achieve carbon neutrality by 2050" and to become "a major player in the energy transition" were ruled to breach consumer protection law because the company was continuing to increase its production and investment in oil and gas, at odds with scientific advice aligned with the Paris Agreement. TotalEnergies was ordered to cease misleading claims, publish the judgment on its website for 180 days, and pay compensation to environmental NGOs.

This ruling is notable because TotalEnergies had the most aggressive carbon pricing integration of any major: it used a minimum carbon price of $40/ton in investment decisions (raised to $100/ton from 2023 onward) and allocated 30% of 2022-2025 investment to "decarbonized energies." Yet the court found these actions were a misleading facade.

### Sources

- [Columbia Climate Law Blog: Greenwashing on Trial](https://blogs.law.columbia.edu/climatechange/2025/12/16/greenwashing-on-trial-the-paris-tribunal-finds-totalenergies-misled-consumers-with-its-carbon-neutrality-claims/)
- [ClientEarth: Historic win against greenwashing](https://www.clientearth.org/latest/press-office/press-releases/historic-win-against-greenwashing-as-court-rules-totalenergies-misled-consumers-on-net-zero/)
- [Climate in the Courts: TotalEnergies Misled Consumers](https://www.climateinthecourts.com/totalenergies-misled-consumers-court-rules-in-historic-greenwashing-judgment/)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_SA moderately
- **Estimated WoE**: +2 to +4 dB for H_SA
- **Diagnosticity**: Moderate. A court finding that TotalEnergies' transition claims were misleading while the company increased fossil fuel production suggests the carbon-pricing-integrated strategy was more performative than substantive. This is consistent with H_SA (strategic accommodation) rather than H_CA (genuine competitive repositioning). However, courts apply consumer protection law, not political economy analysis, so the ruling is indirect evidence about corporate motivations.

## Evidence Item 9: Equinor's CCS/Hydrogen Lobbying and Gas-Producer Positioning

### Description

Equinor -- as Europe's second-largest gas supplier -- has pursued a distinctive strategy that combines carbon pricing advocacy with aggressive promotion of gas, CCS, and "blue" hydrogen:

1. **Gas advocacy**: Equinor states that "Europe should continue to invest in natural gas as the most cost-effective and readily-available solution to meeting its energy security and emission reduction targets."
2. **CCS hub strategy**: Equinor held more meetings with UK ministers to lobby on CCS than any other company, positioning itself to be a leading European CO2 storage provider using Norway's North Sea geology.
3. **Blue hydrogen pipeline**: A leaked document revealed that Equinor's blue hydrogen pipeline (making hydrogen from natural gas + CCS) is the only gas-dependent installation on a draft EU Commission list of "Projects of Common Interest," which would receive fast-track permitting and access to 5.4 billion euros in EU subsidies.
4. **Forced to withdraw CCS claims**: In January 2025, DeSmog reported that Equinor was forced to withdraw a key carbon capture claim, and ClientEarth flagged Equinor as a greenwasher.

### Sources

- [Global Witness: Equinor, Shell and RWE's hydrogen plans](https://globalwitness.org/en/press-releases/equinor-shell-and-rwes-plans-to-flood-europe-with-fossil-hydrogen-may-receive-eu-taxpayer-support/)
- [DeSmog: Norwegian Oil Giant's Plan to Capture UK's Carbon](https://www.desmog.com/2024/10/31/norwegian-oil-giants-plan-to-capture-uks-carbon-is-fraught-with-risks/)
- [DeSmog: Equinor Forced to Withdraw Key Carbon Capture Claim](https://www.desmog.com/2025/01/14/exclusive-norways-equinor-forced-to-withdraw-key-carbon-capture-claim/)
- [ClientEarth: The Greenwashing Files -- Equinor](https://www.clientearth.org/projects/the-greenwashing-files/equinor/)

### Assessment

- **Favors**: H_CA moderately (with H_SA elements)
- **Estimated WoE**: +2 to +3 dB for H_CA
- **Diagnosticity**: Moderate. Equinor's strategy is the strongest case for H_CA among the majors: carbon pricing genuinely creates competitive advantages for its gas and CCS businesses. Norway's geological advantages make CCS a plausible profit center, and higher carbon prices favor gas over coal. However, the leaked document about seeking EU subsidies and the forced withdrawal of CCS claims suggest the strategy is more about capturing regulatory rents (closer to H_SA) than about genuine cost-competitiveness.

### Aggregate

- **Total for H_SA**: ~25 to 37 dB (from E1, E2, E5, E6, E7, E8)
- **Total for H_CA**: ~7 to 10 dB (from E3, E4, E9)
- **Net**: ~15 to 27 dB for H_SA

### Interpretation

The holdout evidence from post-2020 strategy documents, leaked materials, congressional investigations, and lobbying analyses strongly reinforces F&C's posterior favoring H_SA. The most diagnostic items are:

1. **The McCoy sting** (E1): A rare unguarded admission that carbon pricing support is a "talking point," not a genuine policy preference. This alone provides 6-8 dB for H_SA.

2. **The Great Retreat** (E5): The 2023-2025 systematic abandonment of climate commitments by BP, Shell, and Equinor reveals that prior green positioning was contingent and strategic, not driven by genuine commercial enthusiasm for a low-carbon economy.

3. **Congressional investigation** (E6): Internal documents showing private skepticism of climate commitments while publicly supporting carbon pricing directly confirms the H_SA mechanism.

The H_CA evidence is real but narrower: Shell's gas-over-coal positioning and ExxonMobil's CCS business model show that carbon pricing does create some competitive advantages. However, these advantages appear to be secondary benefits rather than primary motivations, and they are often embedded within an overall defensive strategy.

**Key finding for PPC purposes**: The holdout evidence is broadly consistent with F&C's prior assessment that H_SA is more likely than H_CA, and if anything, the post-2020 evidence strengthens the case for H_SA beyond the ~4 dB posterior F&C reported. A researcher conducting a PPC based on these findings would predict that the original analysis may have underweighted the H_SA hypothesis.

### Caveats

1. **Selection bias**: Leaked documents and congressional investigations tend to surface embarrassing material, potentially overrepresenting H_SA-consistent evidence.
2. **Company heterogeneity**: European and US majors may have genuinely different motivations. Equinor's CCS strategy may be more authentically H_CA than ExxonMobil's carbon tax "talking point."
3. **Temporal dynamics**: Motivations may have shifted over time. Early carbon pricing support (pre-2020) may have been more H_CA-motivated, with H_SA elements becoming dominant as regulatory pressure intensified.
4. **Within-company variation**: Different divisions and individuals within a single company may operate under different logics (cf. McCoy vs. Woods at ExxonMobil).
