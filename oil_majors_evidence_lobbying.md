# Post-2020 Lobbying Evidence: Oil Majors and Carbon Pricing

## PPC Holdout Evidence for Fairfield & Charman (2025) Reanalysis

**Hypotheses:**
- **H_SA** (Strategic Accommodation): Support moderate carbon pricing to hedge against / preclude more radical regulation (defensive motive)
- **H_CA** (Competitive Advantage): Support carbon pricing because it creates competitive advantages -- gas over coal, market positioning (offensive motive)

**F&C posterior:** ~4 dB for H_SA (~71% probability). Evidence was mixed. E_3 (CLC carbon tax proposal with liability waiver) was the strongest piece at 12 dB for H_SA.

## Evidence Item 1: Greenpeace Undercover Sting -- Exxon Lobbyist Admits Carbon Tax Is a "Talking Point" (June 2021)

### Description
In June 2021, Greenpeace's investigative unit Unearthed released undercover video of Keith McCoy, ExxonMobil's senior director for federal relations, secretly recorded while speaking to activists posing as headhunters. McCoy stated:

- "Nobody is going to propose a tax on all Americans and the cynical side of me says, yeah, we kind of know that but it gives us a talking point."
- When asked "So it's basically never going to happen right, is the calculation?", McCoy replied: "Yeah. No it's not, it's not."
- "It's an easy talking point to say, I'm for a carbon tax. That's a talking point. That is, in my mind, an effective advocacy tool."
- He also discussed working with "shadow groups" and influencing senators to weaken climate elements of Biden's infrastructure plan.

ExxonMobil CEO Darren Woods condemned the statements and apologized.

### Sources
- [NPR: Exxon Lobbyist Caught On Video](https://www.npr.org/2021/07/01/1012138741/exxon-lobbyist-caught-on-video-talks-about-undermining-bidens-climate-push)
- [Greenpeace Unearthed: Inside Exxon's playbook](https://unearthed.greenpeace.org/2021/06/30/exxon-climate-change-undercover/)
- [CNBC: Exxon lobbyist duped by Greenpeace](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/07/01/exxon-lobbyist-duped-by-greenpeace-says-climate-policy-was-a-ploy-ceo-condemns-statements.html)
- [CBS News: Exxon Mobil Greenpeace video](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/exxon-mobil-greenpeace-video-lobbyist-carbon-tax-talking-point/)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_SA (strongly)**
- **Estimated WoE: +8 to +12 dB for H_SA**
- **Discrimination: HIGH**. This is arguably the single most diagnostic piece of holdout evidence. Under H_SA, Exxon supports carbon pricing precisely because it expects the policy will never pass -- it is a rhetorical shield against more threatening regulation. The lobbyist's admission that carbon tax support is "an effective advocacy tool" and that it "isn't going to happen" is precisely what H_SA predicts: support for a policy known to be politically infeasible, used to deflect pressure. Under H_CA, one would expect genuine enthusiasm about the competitive benefits of carbon pricing (gas displacing coal, CCS revenue streams), not cynical acknowledgment that the policy is a dead letter.

## Evidence Item 2: ExxonMobil Suspended from Climate Leadership Council (August 2021)

### Description
In August 2021, the Climate Leadership Council (CLC) suspended ExxonMobil's membership following the Greenpeace undercover recordings. The CLC initially defended Exxon after the tape dropped in June but conducted an internal review that led to the suspension. Exxon called the decision "disappointing and counterproductive" but said it would continue advocating for carbon pricing.

The CLC continues to operate as of 2025, with BP and ConocoPhillips still listed as corporate partners. Shell also remains involved. ExxonMobil's suspension -- rather than voluntary departure -- is notable.

### Sources
- [Washington Post: Exxon Mobil suspended from the Climate Leadership Council](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/economy/exxon-mobil-suspended-from-the-climate-leadership-council-following-secret-greenpeace-recording/2021/08/06/2db6da3e-f6a6-11eb-9068-bf463c8c74de_story.html)
- [The Hill: Climate alliance suspends Exxon](https://thehill.com/policy/energy-environment/566768-conservation-alliance-suspends-exxon-over-lobbyists-comments-on/)
- [E&E News: Exxon suspended from climate group after sting tape](https://www.eenews.net/articles/exxon-suspended-from-climate-group-after-sting-tape/)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_SA (moderately)**
- **Estimated WoE: +3 to +5 dB for H_SA**
- **Discrimination: MODERATE**. The suspension confirms that Exxon's private stance (carbon tax as a "talking point") was inconsistent with the CLC's stated mission of genuine carbon pricing advocacy. This is more consistent with H_SA (strategic posturing) than H_CA (genuine competitive interest). However, BP and ConocoPhillips remained members, which may suggest more genuine engagement from other firms, slightly tempering the diagnostic value.

## Evidence Item 3: October 2021 House Oversight Committee Hearing on Big Oil

### Description
On October 28, 2021, the CEOs of ExxonMobil (Darren Woods), BP America (David Lawler), Chevron (Michael Wirth), and Shell (Gretchen Watkins), along with API and U.S. Chamber representatives, testified before the House Oversight Committee in a hearing compared to the 1994 tobacco hearings. Key findings:

- Executives were pressed on the disconnect between public climate commitments and internal lobbying behavior.
- Woods argued a government-mandated carbon price would "incentivize every part of the economy to find ways to reduce CO2."
- API President Mike Sommers testified in support of carbon pricing.
- Internal documents obtained by the committee (released in December 2022) revealed API's 2021 Climate Action Framework was organized around "the continued promotion of natural gas in a carbon constrained economy."
- An internal BP email stated BP had "no obligation to minimize GHG emissions" and should only do so where it "makes commercial sense."

### Sources
- [Washington Post: Big Oil CEOs testify before House Oversight Committee](https://www.washingtonpost.com/climate-environment/2021/10/28/oil-executives-testimony-live-updates/)
- [NPR: Oil companies face 'big tobacco moment'](https://www.npr.org/2021/10/28/1049287610/oil-companies-face-big-tobacco-moment-in-congress-over-their-climate-policies)
- [House Oversight Committee: Documents on Greenwashing](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/oversight-committee-releases-new-documents-showing-big-oil-s-greenwashing)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: Ambiguous, but net H_SA (+2 to +4 dB)**
- **Discrimination: MODERATE**. The public testimony (support for carbon pricing) is consistent with both hypotheses. However, the internal documents are more diagnostic: API's framing of carbon pricing as a tool for "promotion of natural gas" partially supports H_CA, while BP's internal admission that it has "no obligation to minimize GHG emissions" is more consistent with H_SA (carbon pricing as a shield, not a genuine business interest). The mixed internal evidence makes this moderately diagnostic.

## Evidence Item 4: API Endorses Carbon Pricing (March 2021) -- With Critical Conditions

### Description
In March 2021, the American Petroleum Institute -- the most powerful oil and gas lobbying group in the U.S. -- officially endorsed carbon pricing for the first time, including it in its "Climate Action Framework." Key details:

- API proposed a starting price of $35-$50 per ton of CO2.
- **Critical condition**: The carbon price should "replace all environmental laws and regulations that are intended to reduce or control carbon and other GHG emissions." This is a regulatory preemption condition -- adopt the carbon tax, eliminate EPA regulatory authority.
- Bloomberg headline: "U.S. Oil Lobby Backs Carbon Fee as Substitute for Regulation."
- API simultaneously continued lobbying against the Inflation Reduction Act's methane fee, opposed building electrification measures, and fought EV subsidies.
- As of 2025, API has not formally withdrawn the carbon pricing endorsement, but neither has it actively lobbied Congress to pass carbon pricing legislation.

### Sources
- [CNBC: API endorses carbon pricing](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/25/climate-change-american-petroleum-institute-endorses-carbon-pricing.html)
- [Bloomberg: U.S. Oil Lobby Backs Carbon Fee as Substitute for Regulation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-03-25/u-s-oil-lobby-backs-carbon-fee-as-substitute-for-regulation)
- [Grist: Oil's biggest lobbying group killed carbon prices. Now it supports them?](https://grist.org/energy/oils-biggest-lobbying-group-killed-carbon-pricing-now-it-supports-them/)
- [InfluenceMap: API's carbon price policy](https://influencemap.org/pressrelease/American-Petroleum-Institute-s-carbon-price-policy-2e62fb8756be1dc5c5e40f9aff65aebf)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_SA (strongly)**
- **Estimated WoE: +6 to +10 dB for H_SA**
- **Discrimination: HIGH**. The regulatory preemption condition is the key discriminator. Under H_SA, supporting carbon pricing *as a substitute* for command-and-control regulation is precisely the strategic accommodation logic: accept a moderate, market-based mechanism to forestall more threatening regulatory interventions (EPA emissions standards, methane rules, EV mandates, building electrification). Under H_CA, one would expect the industry to support carbon pricing *in addition to* other climate policies that accelerate the gas-over-coal transition, not as a replacement for them. The fact that API simultaneously opposed the IRA's methane fee (which would have benefited gas relative to coal by penalizing leaky operations) further undermines H_CA.

## Evidence Item 5: TotalEnergies Exits API over Climate Differences (January 2021)

### Description
TotalEnergies (then Total) announced in January 2021 it would not renew its API membership, citing disagreements over:
- API's opposition to methane regulation (Total supported it)
- API's opposition to EV subsidies
- API's insufficient support for carbon pricing
- Total stated it supports natural gas as a transition fuel to replace coal.

Total invested 20% of CAPEX in further gas development, mainly LNG, described as "the energy of the transition to replace coal in electricity production."

### Sources
- [TotalEnergies press release: Total withdraws from API](https://totalenergies.com/media/news/press-releases/total-withdraws-from-the-american-petroleum-institute)
- [World Oil: Total to drop API membership](https://www.worldoil.com/news/2021/1/15/total-to-drop-api-membership-citing-climate-and-political-differences)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_CA (moderately)**
- **Estimated WoE: -3 to -5 dB (i.e., +3 to +5 dB for H_CA)**
- **Discrimination: MODERATE-HIGH**. TotalEnergies' behavior is a partial outlier that favors H_CA. Its exit from API was motivated by API's insufficient climate ambition -- which makes no sense under pure H_SA (if carbon pricing is just a shield, why leave the shield coalition for not being ambitious enough?). Total explicitly frames natural gas as a competitive transition fuel displacing coal, which is textbook H_CA. However, this evidence primarily speaks to TotalEnergies specifically and may not generalize to the full group of majors.

## Evidence Item 6: InfluenceMap "Big Oil's Real Agenda" Report (2022) -- Dual Advocacy Strategy

### Description
InfluenceMap's 2022 report analyzed the five supermajors' (ExxonMobil, Shell, BP, Chevron, TotalEnergies) lobbying behavior and found:

- Companies spend ~$750 million annually on climate-related PR/communication.
- Only 12% of 2022 CAPEX is dedicated to "low carbon" activities (and some of this includes gas investments).
- None of the companies' production plans are aligned with IEA Net Zero by 2050.
- "None of the companies have aligned their climate policy engagement activities with the goals of the Paris Agreement."
- All retain "a dense and global network of industry associations" that "are actively engaged in opposition to Paris-aligned climate policies."
- BP and Shell hold 42 and 52 industry association memberships respectively, with 21 and 27 of those associations being "oppositional" on climate policy.
- Key finding: the majors follow a **"dual" advocacy strategy** -- publicly call for carbon pricing while "behind the scenes, systematically obstructing the very laws that would enable a meaningful price."

### Sources
- [InfluenceMap: Big Oil's Real Agenda on Climate Change 2022](https://influencemap.org/report/Big-Oil-s-Agenda-on-Climate-Change-2022-19585)
- [InfluenceMap: BP and Shell's Climate Policy Engagement](https://influencemap.org/report/BP-and-Shell-s-Climate-Policy-Engagement-22052)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_SA (strongly)**
- **Estimated WoE: +6 to +8 dB for H_SA**
- **Discrimination: HIGH**. The "dual advocacy strategy" finding -- publicly support carbon pricing while privately opposing the regulatory mechanisms needed to implement it -- is a near-perfect match for H_SA's prediction. If the companies genuinely believed carbon pricing would give them competitive advantages (H_CA), they would not need to systematically obstruct climate policy through industry associations. The disconnect between public support and private obstruction is the signature of strategic accommodation.

## Evidence Item 7: Oil Majors' Massive CCS Investments (2021-2025) -- ExxonMobil's Denbury Acquisition and Northern Lights

### Description
Post-2020, oil majors have made substantial carbon capture and storage (CCS) investments:

**ExxonMobil:**
- April 2021: Proposed $100 billion Houston CCS hub (requiring government funding).
- Established "Low Carbon Solutions" business unit in 2021.
- July 2023: Acquired Denbury Inc. for $4.9 billion, gaining the largest CO2 pipeline network in the U.S. (1,300 miles) and 10 onshore sequestration sites.

**European Majors (Equinor, Shell, TotalEnergies):**
- Northern Lights project (Norway): $3.4 billion CCS infrastructure; world's first commercial third-party CO2 transport and storage facility.
- Phase 2 expansion: NOK 7.5 billion investment approved to increase capacity from 1.5 to 5+ million tonnes CO2/year.
- First CO2 injected in August 2025.
- Equinor aims to capture 25% of the European CCS market by 2035.

**Policy dependence:** BP's 2016 internal presentation (released by House Oversight Committee) stated that CCS "is not economically viable against a free-to-pollute business model" and that a "robust carbon price [would be] needed to stimulate CCUS deployment."

### Sources
- [ExxonMobil: Acquisition of Denbury](https://corporate.exxonmobil.com/news/news-releases/2023/0713_exxonmobil-announces-acquisition-of-denbury)
- [Equinor: Northern Lights project](https://www.equinor.com/energy/northern-lights)
- [Equinor: NOK 7.5 billion investment in Northern Lights Phase 2](https://www.equinor.com/news/20250327-northern-lights-phase-2)
- [Senate Budget Committee Report: Big Oil's Evolving Efforts](https://www.budget.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/fossil_fuel_report1.pdf)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_CA (moderately)**
- **Estimated WoE: -3 to -5 dB (i.e., +3 to +5 dB for H_CA)**
- **Discrimination: MODERATE**. Large-scale CCS investments suggest at least some companies are positioning to profit from carbon pricing (the CCS business model depends on it), which is consistent with H_CA's competitive advantage logic. However, H_SA can partially accommodate this evidence: CCS investments may serve as another defensive strategy (demonstrating "responsible" behavior to forestall regulation) or may reflect rent-seeking from government subsidies (45Q credits, IRA funding) rather than genuine competitive positioning. The dependence on government subsidies weakens the H_CA interpretation somewhat, but the scale of investment ($4.9B for Denbury alone) suggests more than pure posturing.

## Evidence Item 8: BP and Shell Retreat from Renewables (2023-2025)

### Description
In 2023-2024, European oil majors dramatically scaled back climate commitments:

**BP:**
- Reduced 2030 production cut target from 40% to 25%.
- CEO Murray Auchincloss stated BP had gone "too far, too fast" and its faith in green energy was "misplaced."
- Cut transition energy investment from ~$7B to $1.5-2B per year.
- Increased oil and gas investment by ~20% to $10B/year.
- In February 2025, BP dropped climate targets entirely in a full pivot back to oil and gas.

**Shell:**
- Froze spending on renewable energy.
- Pulled out of wind, hydrogen projects.
- Sold refineries and softened 2030 carbon reduction targets.

**Overall:**
- BP, Shell, and Equinor reduced low-carbon spending by 8% in 2024.
- None of the supermajors' production plans align with IEA Net Zero by 2050.

### Sources
- [Grist: Why are BP, Shell, and Exxon suddenly backing off their climate promises?](https://grist.org/economics/bp-exxon-shell-backing-off-climate-promises/)
- [Fast Company: BP and Shell backtracked on climate goals in 2024](https://www.fastcompany.com/91253071/bp-shell-backtracked-climate-goals-2024-heres-why)
- [Al Jazeera: BP drops climate targets in pivot back to oil and gas](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/2/26/bp-drops-climate-targets-in-switch-back-to-oil-and-gas)
- [IEEFA: BP's retreat is a reality check for investors](https://ieefa.org/resources/bps-retreat-reality-check-investors)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_SA (moderately to strongly)**
- **Estimated WoE: +4 to +7 dB for H_SA**
- **Discrimination: MODERATE-HIGH**. Under H_CA, companies that see genuine competitive advantage in carbon pricing and the energy transition would be expected to sustain or increase transition investments. The dramatic retreat suggests these investments were not driven by competitive conviction but by external pressure (investor ESG demands, regulatory threats) that has since eased. When the political/market environment shifted (energy crisis, high oil profits, reduced regulatory pressure under Trump administration), the companies reverted to core fossil fuel business. This pattern -- invest in transition under pressure, retreat when pressure eases -- is more consistent with H_SA's defensive posturing than H_CA's offensive positioning.

## Evidence Item 9: ConocoPhillips -- Supports Carbon Price "as Replacement" for All Climate Regulation

### Description
ConocoPhillips joined the Carbon Pricing Leadership Coalition (CPLC) in 2021 and publicly advocates for carbon pricing. However, its 2021 Sustainability Report states that a federal carbon tax "should replace all environmental laws and regulations that are intended to reduce or control carbon and other GHG emissions." ConocoPhillips simultaneously:
- Ranked 4th among the most negative and influential companies on climate policy globally (InfluenceMap, November 2022).
- Increased lobbying spending to $8.7 million in 2022 (nearly doubling from 2021), primarily focused on the Willow oil project in Alaska.

### Sources
- [ConocoPhillips: Carbon price policy engagement](https://www.conocophillips.com/sustainability/managing-climate-related-risks/public-policy/carbon-pricing/)
- [InfluenceMap: ConocoPhillips and Climate Change Policy](https://influencemap.org/briefing/ConocoPhillips-Influence-on-Climate-Policy-21993)
- [OpenSecrets: ConocoPhillips lobbying 2022](https://www.opensecrets.org/news/2023/03/conocophillips-lobbying-2022-willow/)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_SA (strongly)**
- **Estimated WoE: +5 to +8 dB for H_SA**
- **Discrimination: HIGH**. The explicit "replacement" condition is the key. Supporting carbon pricing *only if* it eliminates all other climate regulation is classic strategic accommodation: accept a moderate mechanism to remove more threatening regulatory tools. Under H_CA, one would expect support for carbon pricing based on its business merits, regardless of what happens to other regulations.

## Evidence Item 10: EU Lobbying -- European Majors Support EU ETS While Weakening Ambition

### Description
European oil majors (Shell, BP, TotalEnergies, Equinor) publicly supported EU ETS reforms under the Fit for 55 package (2021-2023). However:

- Industry lobbying resulted in weaker-than-proposed reforms, maintaining higher free emissions allowance allocation to heavy industry.
- Shell submitted comments on California's Cap-and-Trade favoring less ambitious cap trajectories.
- Companies lobbied against gas demand-reducing measures in the Fit for 55 package while supporting carbon pricing in principle.
- Equinor identified "partial misalignment" with API and CAPP on climate policy but remained a member of both.

On CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism):
- Energy-intensive industries lobbied heavily against CBAM provisions.
- Oil and gas products (crude, refined petroleum) were initially excluded, with inclusion postponed until 2026-2030.

### Sources
- [InfluenceMap: EU ETS Reform](https://europe.influencemap.org/policy/EU-ETS-Reform-1469)
- [Shell: Climate and Energy Transition Lobbying Report 2022](https://reports.shell.com/climate-and-energy-transition-lobbying-report/2022/assessments/american-petroleum-institute-api.html)
- [InfluenceMap: EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism](https://europe.influencemap.org/policy/EU-Carbon-Border-Adjustment-Mechanism-430)

### Diagnostic Assessment
- **Favors: H_SA (moderately)**
- **Estimated WoE: +3 to +5 dB for H_SA**
- **Discrimination: MODERATE**. Supporting carbon pricing in principle while lobbying to reduce its ambition and scope is more consistent with H_SA (defensive, controlled exposure) than H_CA (which would predict advocacy for more ambitious carbon prices to maximize competitive advantage). However, some of the lobbying against demand-reducing measures could also reflect H_CA logic (protecting gas markets specifically).

## Summary Assessment

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greenpeace/Exxon sting: carbon tax is a "talking point" | H_SA | +8 to +12 | HIGH |
| 2 | Exxon suspended from CLC | H_SA | +3 to +5 | MODERATE |
| 3 | House Oversight hearings and internal documents | H_SA | +2 to +4 | MODERATE |
| 4 | API endorses carbon pricing with regulatory preemption condition | H_SA | +6 to +10 | HIGH |
| 5 | TotalEnergies exits API, frames gas as competitive transition fuel | H_CA | -3 to -5 | MODERATE-HIGH |
| 6 | InfluenceMap "dual advocacy" strategy finding | H_SA | +6 to +8 | HIGH |
| 7 | Massive CCS investments (Denbury, Northern Lights) | H_CA | -3 to -5 | MODERATE |
| 8 | BP/Shell retreat from renewables (2023-2025) | H_SA | +4 to +7 | MODERATE-HIGH |
| 9 | ConocoPhillips: carbon price as "replacement" for all climate regulation | H_SA | +5 to +8 | HIGH |
| 10 | EU lobbying: support ETS while weakening ambition | H_SA | +3 to +5 | MODERATE |

### Net Assessment

**Aggregate estimated WoE: approximately +25 to +40 dB for H_SA**

The holdout evidence strongly reinforces F&C's original posterior favoring H_SA. The most diagnostic pieces are:

1. **The Greenpeace sting** (Evidence 1): A senior Exxon lobbyist's admission that carbon tax support is a cynical "talking point" for a policy they know "isn't going to happen" is devastating for H_CA and almost perfectly predicted by H_SA.

2. **The regulatory preemption condition** (Evidence 4, 9): Both API and ConocoPhillips explicitly condition their carbon pricing support on elimination of other climate regulations. This transforms "support for carbon pricing" from a policy preference into a defensive strategy to preempt more threatening regulatory tools.

3. **The "dual advocacy" pattern** (Evidence 6): Systematic public support for carbon pricing combined with private obstruction of climate policy through industry associations is the signature behavioral pattern predicted by H_SA.

The main counterevidence comes from TotalEnergies' behavior (Evidence 5) and CCS investments (Evidence 7), which suggest at least some companies see genuine competitive advantages. However, these items are less diagnostic because:
- TotalEnergies may be an outlier among the majors (its gas-heavy portfolio makes H_CA particularly applicable to this specific company).
- CCS investments depend heavily on government subsidies and may serve dual purposes (genuine positioning AND defensive reputation management).

### Implications for PPC

If F&C's posterior was ~4 dB for H_SA, and the holdout evidence aggregates to approximately +25 to +40 dB for H_SA, the updated posterior would be overwhelmingly in favor of H_SA. The holdout evidence does not generate a discrepancy -- it confirms and strengthens the original posterior direction.

However, a more nuanced PPC would note that the evidence is **company-heterogeneous**: the data strongly favor H_SA for ExxonMobil, ConocoPhillips, and (to some extent) BP and Shell, while TotalEnergies and Equinor show patterns more consistent with H_CA. This suggests the case might benefit from company-level rather than industry-level hypotheses.
