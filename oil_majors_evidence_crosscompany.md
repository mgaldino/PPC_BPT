# Cross-Company Comparative Evidence: Oil Majors and Carbon Pricing (Post-2020)

## PPC for Fairfield & Charman (2025) Reanalysis of Vormedal et al. (2020)

**Hypotheses:**
- **H_SA** (Strategic Accommodation): Support carbon pricing to hedge against more radical regulation (defensive). Predicts UNIFORM support regardless of gas portfolio; support tracks regulatory threat.
- **H_CA** (Competitive Advantage): Support carbon pricing because gas benefits over coal (offensive). Predicts DIFFERENTIAL support correlated with gas portfolio share.

**F&C posterior:** ~4 dB for H_SA (~71% probability).

---

## 1. Gas Portfolio vs. Carbon Pricing Enthusiasm

### 1.1 Gas Production Shares (approximate, 2023 data)

| Company | Gas Share (approx.) | Region | Carbon Pricing Stance |
|---------|-------------------|--------|----------------------|
| Shell | ~86% gas/14% liquids | European | Strong advocate; direct lobbying for EU ETS, publishes annual lobbying report |
| Equinor | ~48% gas/52% liquids | European (Norway) | Strong advocate; supports EU ETS, CBAM, EU-UK ETS linking |
| TotalEnergies | Target ~50% gas by 2030 (currently ~40-45%); 3rd largest LNG company globally | European (France) | Strong advocate; supports CBAM, Fit for 55, internal carbon price of $100/t |
| BP | ~55% gas (6.9 bcf/d gas vs ~1 Mbbl/d oil) | European (UK) | Was strongest advocate ("beyond petroleum"); now retreating |
| ExxonMobil | ~35% gas/65% liquids | US | Publicly supports carbon tax; lobbyist admitted it is a "talking point" (2021 undercover video) |
| ConocoPhillips | ~29% gas/51% oil/16% NGL/4% bitumen | US | Supports Baker-Shultz carbon dividend plan; founding CLC member; still in CLC as of 2024 |
| Chevron | Oil-heavy (not in CLC) | US | Verbally supports "global price on carbon" but opposes specific state-level carbon policies (e.g., California cap and trade) |

### 1.2 Assessment

The data shows a rough positive correlation between gas share and carbon pricing advocacy intensity: Shell (86% gas) is the most active lobbyist for carbon pricing, while Chevron (oil-heavy, not in CLC) offers only rhetorical support. This pattern is consistent with H_CA.

However, ConocoPhillips (only 29% gas, oil-heavy) is a founding CLC member and active carbon pricing advocate, which is anomalous under H_CA. F&C flagged this as a puzzle, and the post-2020 data confirms ConocoPhillips has maintained this stance -- still in the CLC as of March 2024, joined the Carbon Pricing Leadership Coalition (CPLC) in 2021, and continues bipartisan Congressional advocacy.

**Evidence assessment:**
- Rough gas-share/advocacy correlation: **Mildly favors H_CA (~1-2 dB)**
- ConocoPhillips anomaly (oil-heavy but strong advocate): **Favors H_SA (~2-3 dB)**
- Net assessment of this domain: **Ambiguous, ~0 dB net** (the correlation and counter-example roughly cancel)

## 2. European vs. US Majors: Geography and Regulatory Pressure

### 2.1 InfluenceMap Climate Policy Engagement Scores (2022-2024)

| Company | Score | Region |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Shell | C- | European |
| BP | C- | European |
| TotalEnergies | C- | European |
| ExxonMobil | D | US |
| Chevron | D- | US |
| ConocoPhillips | (not scored in top tier) | US |

European majors consistently score higher (C-) than US majors (D/D-) on climate policy engagement. This is a clear geographic split.

### 2.2 Specific Behavioral Differences

- **European majors** (Shell, BP, TotalEnergies, Equinor): All publicly support EU ETS, CBAM, and carbon pricing mechanisms. Shell publishes annual Climate and Energy Transition Lobbying Reports. Equinor co-signed a 2025 letter supporting EU-UK ETS linking.
- **US majors**: ExxonMobil publicly supports carbon tax but was suspended from CLC in 2021 after lobbyist admitted it was a "talking point" (i.e., a position taken precisely because it would never pass). Chevron supports carbon pricing in principle but opposes specific implementations (California cap-and-trade). ConocoPhillips is the exception -- active advocate despite being US-based.

### 2.3 Assessment

The European/US split in advocacy intensity maps onto regulatory pressure: European companies face the EU Green Deal, Fit for 55, EU ETS, and CBAM, while US companies face weaker federal climate policy (especially post-Trump 2024 election). H_SA predicts this geographic divergence (more regulatory threat => more support as hedging strategy). H_CA predicts support based on gas portfolio regardless of geography.

The ExxonMobil undercover video (2021) is particularly diagnostic: a lobbyist explicitly stated that Exxon supports a carbon tax as a "talking point" because it knows Republicans will never pass it -- this is a textbook H_SA strategy (appearing supportive without bearing real costs).

**Evidence assessment:**
- European/US split in advocacy intensity: **Favors H_SA (~2-3 dB)**
- ExxonMobil "talking point" admission: **Strongly favors H_SA (~3-5 dB)**
- ConocoPhillips as US exception (weakens geographic pattern): **Mildly favors H_CA (~1 dB)**
- Net assessment: **Favors H_SA, ~3-4 dB**

## 3. Companies that WITHDREW or REDUCED Climate/Carbon Pricing Support

### 3.1 The 2023-2025 Climate Retreat

A striking post-2020 development is the simultaneous retreat of European majors from climate commitments:

**BP (most dramatic retreat):**
- Feb 2023: Bernard Looney lowered emissions reduction target from 40% to 25% by 2030
- Sept 2023: Looney resigned
- Feb 2025: New CEO Murray Auchincloss scrapped renewables targets entirely, increased oil/gas investment to $10B/year, cut low-carbon investment from $4B to $0.8B/year, halted 18 hydrogen projects
- Key quote from Auchincloss: "The two priorities in all countries where we operate is affordable energy and reliable energy... they need oil and gas."

**Shell:**
- Mid-2023: New CEO Wael Sawan abandoned $100M/year carbon offset program
- 2023: Dismissed target to reduce oil production by 1-2% annually
- 2024: Abandoned 2035 emissions intensity target, weakened 2030 goal
- Reduced low-carbon capex proportion from 23% to ~20%

**Equinor:**
- 2025: Scrapped pledge to devote >50% of capex to renewables by 2030
- Reduced 2030 net carbon intensity target from 20% to 15-20%
- Cut renewables investment by ~50%
- CEO Opedal: "The opportunity set for high-value growth is more limited than we had anticipated"

**TotalEnergies:** Relatively more consistent -- maintains significant LNG/gas expansion strategy and continues supporting CBAM and EU ETS.

### 3.2 Did Retreats Correlate with Regulatory Pressure or Competitive Position?

Multiple sources identify the primary drivers:
1. **Profitability**: Low returns on renewables vs. high oil/gas profits post-Ukraine war
2. **Competitive pressure**: European majors' share prices lagged US rivals (Exxon, Chevron) that stayed focused on oil/gas
3. **Cost/supply chain**: Offshore wind and hydrogen projects faced spiraling costs
4. **Political shifts**: Trump 2024 election, weakening of IRA implementation, shift in government priorities

Critically, **regulatory pressure did NOT decrease** in Europe -- the EU ETS, CBAM, and Fit for 55 remained in force and were strengthened. The retreat occurred DESPITE continued regulatory pressure, driven by profitability and competitive dynamics.

### 3.3 Assessment

This evidence is complex:
- The retreat is NOT correlated with reduced regulatory threat (which would favor H_SA). Instead, it is driven by competitive/financial factors, which is more consistent with H_CA logic (support/oppose based on business advantage).
- However, the retreat was from general climate commitments (renewables, emissions targets, offsets), NOT from carbon pricing support specifically. Shell continues to publish lobbying reports advocating for carbon pricing. BP and Equinor still verbally support carbon pricing. The retreat is about investment allocation, not policy advocacy.
- The simultaneous nature of the retreat across all European majors (regardless of gas share) is somewhat consistent with H_SA (common factor driving uniform behavior).

**Evidence assessment:**
- Retreat driven by profitability, not reduced regulation: **Mildly favors H_CA (~1-2 dB)**
- Retreat was from renewables/climate targets, NOT from carbon pricing advocacy per se: **Neutral (~0 dB)**
- Simultaneous retreat across European majors regardless of gas share: **Mildly favors H_SA (~1 dB)**
- Net assessment: **Roughly neutral, ~0-1 dB toward H_CA**

## 4. Differential Behavior on Specific Policies

### 4.1 EU CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism)

- **TotalEnergies**: Explicitly supports CBAM in 2023 and 2024 reports
- **Equinor**: Supports CBAM in June 2023 comments and May 2024 meeting with EU policymakers
- **Shell**: Supports carbon pricing broadly; detailed position in annual lobbying reports
- Oil refining not yet covered by current CBAM scope but expected to be included by 2030

H_CA prediction: Gas-heavy companies should support CBAM more (gas benefits from carbon pricing vs. coal). The data shows European majors (with higher gas shares) support CBAM, but this confounds with geography (CBAM is an EU policy). **Low discrimination: ~1 dB either direction.**

### 4.2 US Inflation Reduction Act (Subsidies vs. Carbon Price)

- The IRA chose subsidies and tax credits rather than carbon pricing -- a fundamentally different mechanism
- Oil majors (especially ExxonMobil, Chevron) pivoted to embrace IRA subsidies for carbon capture (CCS/CCUS)
- ExxonMobil CEO Darren Woods: "There's a lot of activity in this space [carbon capture], a lot of interest, particularly with the IRA. I think we're very well positioned there."
- Chevron expanded its planned Texas CCS hub 3x in response to IRA incentives
- This pivot from carbon pricing advocacy to subsidy-capture is consistent with **both** hypotheses: H_SA (any climate policy that avoids more radical regulation) and H_CA (capture subsidies to enhance competitive position in CCS)

**Evidence assessment: Low discrimination, ~0 dB**

### 4.3 ExxonMobil's CLC Suspension

- ExxonMobil was a CLC founding member
- In June 2021, Greenpeace's undercover video showed lobbyist Keith McCoy admitting carbon tax support was a "talking point" and "advocacy tool" -- Exxon backed it precisely because it would never pass
- CLC suspended ExxonMobil in August 2021
- ExxonMobil has NOT been reinstated (as of March 2024)
- ExxonMobil reportedly refused to rejoin, stating the CLC was trying to "induce us to write them a big check"
- Despite this, ExxonMobil continues to publicly state support for carbon pricing in official communications

This is highly diagnostic: the gap between public support and private strategy directly supports H_SA (carbon pricing support as strategic positioning, not genuine competitive preference).

**Evidence assessment: Strongly favors H_SA (~4-5 dB)**

## 5. Key Discriminating Case: ConocoPhillips

### 5.1 Post-2020 Position

- **Still in CLC**: As of March 2024 CLC partners list, ConocoPhillips remains a partner
- **Expanded advocacy**: Joined Carbon Pricing Leadership Coalition (CPLC) as Private Sector Partner in 2021
- **Continued lobbying**: Over 100 bipartisan Congressional meetings on carbon dividends
- **Baker-Shultz Plan**: Continues to support the four-pillar approach (gradually increasing carbon price, carbon dividends, border carbon adjustments, regulatory simplification)
- **Alliance to Save Energy**: Joined to support energy efficiency policies
- **But opposed IRA methane fee**: In March 2024 comments to EPA, ConocoPhillips opposed the methane fee in the Inflation Reduction Act

### 5.2 Marathon Oil Acquisition (2024)

- ConocoPhillips completed $22.5B acquisition of Marathon Oil in November 2024
- Marathon Oil's portfolio is concentrated in four US resource plays plus an integrated gas business in Equatorial Guinea
- This acquisition reinforces ConocoPhillips' oil-heavy profile

### 5.3 Assessment

ConocoPhillips remains the most puzzling case:
- **Oil-heavy (29% gas)** but one of the most active carbon pricing advocates
- **US-based** (facing weaker regulatory pressure than European peers) but more engaged than other US majors
- **Opposition to methane fee** while supporting carbon tax suggests nuance: they support carbon pricing mechanisms that come with regulatory simplification (replacing EPA regulations with market mechanisms) but oppose incremental fees on top of existing regulation

This pattern is most consistent with **H_SA with a regulatory-simplification motive**: ConocoPhillips supports carbon pricing as a way to REPLACE more burdensome command-and-control regulation (the fourth pillar of the Baker-Shultz Plan is "regulatory simplification"). The methane fee opposition is revealing -- they oppose additive regulation but support a carbon price that would substitute for it.

**Evidence assessment: Favors H_SA (~2-3 dB)**

## 6. Summary Evidence Table

| Evidence Item | Description | Favors | Estimated WoE (dB) | Discrimination |
|--------------|-------------|--------|--------------------:|----------------|
| E*1: Gas-share/advocacy correlation | Rough positive correlation between gas production share and carbon pricing advocacy intensity | H_CA | +1.5 | Low-Medium |
| E*2: ConocoPhillips anomaly | Oil-heavy US company is active carbon pricing advocate; founding CLC member through 2024 | H_SA | +2.5 | Medium |
| E*3: European/US geographic split | European majors score C- on InfluenceMap vs D/D- for US majors; tracks regulatory pressure | H_SA | +2.5 | Medium |
| E*4: ExxonMobil "talking point" | Lobbyist admitted carbon tax support is PR strategy because it will never pass | H_SA | +4.5 | High |
| E*5: Climate retreat 2023-2025 | BP, Shell, Equinor retreated from climate targets but NOT from carbon pricing advocacy | Ambiguous | ~0 | Low |
| E*6: Climate retreat drivers | Retreat driven by profitability/competition, not reduced regulatory pressure | H_CA | +1.5 | Low-Medium |
| E*7: CBAM support by European majors | TotalEnergies, Equinor support CBAM; confounded with geography | Ambiguous | ~0.5 | Low |
| E*8: IRA pivot to CCS subsidies | US majors embraced IRA carbon capture subsidies; consistent with both hypotheses | Ambiguous | ~0 | Very Low |
| E*9: COP methane fee opposition | ConocoPhillips opposes additive methane fee but supports substitutive carbon tax | H_SA | +2.0 | Medium |
| E*10: ExxonMobil CLC suspension | Suspended 2021, not reinstated; refused to rejoin; still claims support publicly | H_SA | +3.0 | Medium-High |

### Cumulative Assessment

**Net WoE from cross-company evidence: ~5-7 dB in favor of H_SA**

The cross-company comparative evidence, taken as a whole, moderately supports H_SA over H_CA. The most diagnostic items are:

1. **ExxonMobil's revealed preferences** (E*4, E*10): The gap between public positioning and private strategy is the strongest single piece of evidence for H_SA. A company genuinely motivated by competitive advantage (H_CA) would not need to treat carbon pricing as a mere "talking point."

2. **Geographic pattern** (E*3): The European/US split in advocacy intensity tracks regulatory pressure more than gas portfolio composition, supporting H_SA.

3. **ConocoPhillips** (E*2, E*9): This case is the most puzzling under H_CA (oil-heavy but advocates) and most coherent under H_SA with regulatory-simplification logic.

The main evidence for H_CA is the rough gas-share correlation (E*1) and the profit-driven nature of the climate retreat (E*6), but these are weaker and lower-discrimination items.

**Combined with F&C's prior of ~4 dB for H_SA, the updated posterior would be approximately 9-11 dB for H_SA (~89-93% probability).**
